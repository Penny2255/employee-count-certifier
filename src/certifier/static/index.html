        How certification works

        Every figure comes from a public source we can show you, chosen by a fixed policy and scored for confidence.

        The source hierarchy

        1. 1
          Primary

          Annual reports and official filings: 10-K and 20-F, government financial reports; then the company's official Corporate Fact Sheet, nonprofit Form 990 and university IPEDS.

        2. 2
          Secondary

          What the organization publishes or says: press releases, its website, its LinkedIn description, executives quoted in reputable press.

        3. 3
          Tertiary

          Data providers such as ZoomInfo, Crunchbase and Revelio.

        4. 4
          Unranked

          Everything else. Used only as context, never as the answer.

        The confidence scale

        5 · Certified, corroborated
        A recent official filing, confirmed by an independent source.
        4 · Certified
        A recent official filing.
        3 · Supported
        An older official filing, or a company statement others back up.
        2 · Estimated
        A company statement on its own, or consistent data-provider estimates.
        1 · Unverified
        Thin, old or conflicting evidence. Treat as a lead, not a fact.
        0 · Unattained
        No usable public source was found.

        What each source type means

        Every source on a certification is labelled with its tier, category and type. Open a type for a plain explanation.

        __GLOSSARY__

        Open beta

        • Free to try. Anyone with the link can search, with no sign-up. Each visitor gets about 10 new lookups a day; the counter resets at midnight Pacific.
        • Cached and sample results are always free. A certification is reused for 90 days, and the sample results never count.
        • Shared capacity. When the beta's daily capacity is used up, new lookups pause until tomorrow; cached and sample results still work.

        Principles

        • The highest source wins. An official filing outranks a press release, which outranks a data provider.
        • Corroboration must be independent. Copies of the same figure don't count as confirmation.
        • Recency matters. Older figures score lower, and newer evidence is shown alongside.
        • Every adjustment is explained. Each score change and each excluded source comes with a reason.
        • Hard cases get human review. Ambiguous scope and conflicting sources are flagged for a person to check.

        Privacy

        No sign-up: GTM Data Certifier is an open beta. We don't ask who you are.

        Fair use: to share the beta fairly, each visitor has a small daily allowance. We key it on a hash of your IP address, salted with a random value that changes every day; the IP address itself is never stored, and the hash is used only for these limits. If you connect Claude, the connection keeps a keyed hash of the IP you signed in from, so it shares your allowance.

        What we store for quality review: the company you searched for, the certification result, and your rating and comment if you leave one. Don't enter personal information in comments.

        In your browser only: your last 10 lookups. Clearing your browser's site data removes them.

        About

        GTM Data Certifier · Giving you transparency & trust to take real action

        GTM Data Certifier finds the most authoritative public figure for an organization's global headcount, shows exactly where it came from (down to the page), and scores how far you can rely on it. Machines do the deep sweep; the policy, the evidence and the hard calls stay visible for humans to judge.

        Open beta

        GTM Data Certifier is in open beta: no sign-up, a fair daily allowance per visitor, and free cached and sample results. Tell us how we did with the rating on every result.

        How it's built

        The certifier is a custom Model Context Protocol (MCP) server built with Claude Code. Claude does the research and extraction: it searches the public web, reads filings and pages, and records each figure with its quote, date and location. The certification rules (which source wins, how confidence is scored, what gets flagged) are deterministic, tested code that follows a published policy, so the same evidence always gets the same answer.
